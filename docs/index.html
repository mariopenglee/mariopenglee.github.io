cofounder of condense.chat. making powerful AI accessible to everyone, and safe when it gets there.

I'm cofounder and chief scientist at condense.chat, which compresses what coding agents send to the model so heavy AI workloads cost less (up to about 70% off long sessions, with basically the same output). I'm also CEO of Pocketful, which brings WhatsApp AI agents to restaurant owners in Argentina. Same mission for both: AI that everyone can actually afford and use.

My background is in linguistics, computer science and psychology (UCLA, Phi Beta Kappa). What I'm most interested in is how minds, human and machine, represent things, and how understanding that makes AI cheaper and safer. These days that means open-source interpretability research at EleutherAI.

Before condense I was a senior AI engineer at nexos.ai, where I built the agent layer, the memory model behind nexos.heartbeat, and a sandboxed code interpreter.

writing

  • Kids vs LLMs at telling a story (2026-10-18): Give a five year old and a language model the same wordless picture book. In 2024 the kids kept track of the characters far better than GPT-4. Two years of models later, the best ones tell the story just like the kids do.
  • cyclediffusion: can a picture be a bottleneck? (2026-10-17): I trained a diffusion model so a captioner could read its pictures back into the original sentence. Instead of inventing a secret visual code, it collapsed into texture, and the reason is the reader you train against.
  • Do sparse autoencoders capture manifolds? (2026-07-15): A concept like "year" lives on a curved manifold, and a single sparse autoencoder often shatters it instead of capturing it whole. Training several SAEs that differ only in random seed and pooling them tiles the manifold back together, right where one SAE dilutes it.
  • block-GRPO: doing RL on a diffusion language model (2026-05-09): GRPO needs a log-probability that diffusion LLMs can't give you cheaply. The fix is to use the model's own block-autoregressive structure, and a few bugs later it beat vanilla GRPO by about 10x on tail reward.
  • Building nexos.heartbeat: the infinite context problem (2026-03-01): An always-on AI twin doesn't have a context window problem, it has an infinite context problem. Information theory, the design space, and why our memory is a git repository.
  • GPT-2, IOI, and the sphex problem (2025-05-21): A wasp can look like it understands what it's doing right up until you move one thing. I poked at GPT-2's indirect-object circuit the same way, to see whether it understood the task or just had a routine that usually works.
  • Does parameter sharing help? a multitask recommender experiment (2025-05-10): I built the same recommender four times to find out whether sharing parameters across tasks helps. It turned out the loss weighting mattered more than the sharing, which is a known and slightly annoying axis.
  • Starting a blog (2025-05-01): Why this page exists, and what to expect.
